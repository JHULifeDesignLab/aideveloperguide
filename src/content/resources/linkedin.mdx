## Why LinkedIn matters for AI roles
</div>

Recruiters for AI and engineering roles actively search LinkedIn by keyword — Vertex AI, Bedrock, RAG, LangChain, Claude Code. If these don't appear in your profile, you won't show up. Your profile isn't a resume copy-paste; it's a searchable document that should reflect every credentialed thing you've done.

---

<div id="skills-section">
## Skills section
</div>

Add every specific tool and service you actually used. LinkedIn's skills section directly feeds recruiter search filters.

**Add these as you complete each path:**

- Generative AI
- Large Language Models (LLMs)
- Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
- Prompt Engineering
- Google Cloud Platform / Vertex AI
- Amazon Bedrock / AWS
- Microsoft Azure / Azure OpenAI Service
- LangChain
- Vector Databases
- Responsible AI / AI Ethics
- Claude Code / AI-assisted development

Don't add skills you can't talk about in an interview. But if you completed the path, you can talk about it.

<div id="certifications-section">
## Certifications section
</div>

LinkedIn has a dedicated certifications section — use it. Each badge from this guide belongs here:

1. Go to your profile → **Add section** → **Licenses & Certifications**
2. Fill in the credential name, issuing organization, and date
3. Paste the credential URL if you have one (Google and AWS both provide shareable badge links)

This is one of the highest-signal things you can do. Recruiters filter for verified credentials.

<div id="featured-section">
## Featured section
</div>

Pin at least one project. The featured section appears near the top of your profile and is the first thing most people click after reading your headline.

What to pin:
- A GitHub repo for a project you built in this guide
- A Loom walkthrough of a working RAG app or chatbot
- A blog post or LinkedIn article explaining something you learned (even a short one)

<div id="about-section">
## About section
</div>

Write 3–4 sentences max. Formula:

> I'm a [year] student at [school] studying [field]. I've been building with [specific tools] and recently [completed X / built Y / earned Z]. I'm interested in [specific area] and looking for [internship/role type].

Keep it current. Update it every time you finish a new path.

---

<div id="posting-strategy">
## Posting strategy
</div>

You don't need to post constantly. One post per project is enough:

- Screenshot of your skill badge with 2–3 sentences about what you learned
- A short write-up of a problem you hit and how you solved it
- A link to your GitHub project with a one-line explanation of what it does

These posts get indexed by LinkedIn search AND signal to your network that you're actively building things.
