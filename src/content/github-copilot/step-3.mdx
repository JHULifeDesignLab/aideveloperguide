## Goal
Lock in the habits that keep you the engineer, and finish the three-tool comparison that turns tool use into an interview answer.

---

## The discipline (same rules, highest stakes here)

Copilot's frictionlessness is its risk: suggestions arrive unbidden, mid-thought, in your own style. The rules from across this site apply with extra force:

1. **Read what you accept.** Every time. The failure mode isn't bad suggestions — it's good-looking ones absorbed on autopilot.
2. **Run the tests before and after.** An assistant that writes code and the habit of verifying it are a package deal.
3. **Don't let it do your learning.** If a suggestion uses something you don't understand, stop and ask chat to explain it — comprehension is what you're actually building your degree out of. (Also check each course's AI policy; "the tool suggested it" is not an academic-integrity defense.)
4. **Know its blind spots by name.** Yours, from Step 1's exercise — most likely: your project's custom abstractions, newer library versions, and anything requiring whole-repo reasoning.

## Check yourself

Done when: your acceptance rate has a reason behind it (you reject things, and know why), your tests run as part of your acceptance habit, and you can deliver the three-tool answer above in your own words with your own examples. That's the whole point of this guide — not Copilot mastery, but demonstrated judgment with the entire category.
