## Goal
Get Copilot running free through GitHub Education, and learn inline completions as a skill rather than a slot machine.

---

## Free access, properly

1. Apply for the [GitHub Student Developer Pack](https://education.github.com/pack) with your .edu email and proof of enrollment — approval typically takes a few days, and the pack includes far more than Copilot
2. Once verified, enable Copilot from your GitHub settings (the Education benefit activates the paid tier at no cost)
3. Install the **GitHub Copilot** extension in VS Code (or your JetBrains IDE) and sign in with GitHub

If you can't wait for verification, a limited free tier exists — but as a student there's no reason to pay: verify once, benefit all degree long. Current plan details live in [the official docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot).

---

## Completions: the calibration skill

Copilot's core feature is the gray ghost text: it watches what you type and proposes what comes next — a line, a block, sometimes a whole function. `Tab` accepts; keep typing to reject.

The skill is the same one our [Cursor guide](/aideveloperguide/cursor) teaches, and it transfers both ways:

- **Write the comment or signature first.** Copilot completes from context — a clear function name, typed parameters, and a one-line docstring produce dramatically better suggestions than a blank line.
- **Accept in small pieces.** A line you've read beats a block you've skimmed.
- **Treat every acceptance as a micro code review.** If you wouldn't approve it in a PR, don't Tab it into your file.

## Check yourself

Done when Copilot runs free on your account, you've consciously rejected at least a few plausible-but-wrong suggestions, and you can name the two contexts where its suggestions are weakest for you.
