## Goal
Get Cursor installed with your familiar setup intact, and learn the two features you'll use hundreds of times a day: Tab and inline edit.

---

## Install and import (10 minutes)

1. Download from [cursor.com](https://cursor.com) (macOS, Windows, Linux)
2. On first launch, choose **Import from VS Code** — extensions, themes, keybindings, and settings all carry over, because Cursor is a VS Code fork
3. Sign in — a free account works to start
4. Students: check [cursor.com/students](https://cursor.com/students) and verify with your .edu email if the education offer is available to you

The "it's just VS Code" part matters: there's no new editor to learn, so the switching cost is nearly zero, and if you walk away, your workflow walks with you.

---

## Tab: autocomplete that edits

Cursor's **Tab** is not the single-token autocomplete you know. It predicts *edits*: complete the line, rewrite the next several, or jump you to the next place in the file that needs the same change. Rename a variable, and Tab starts offering the matching updates as you move.

The skill is calibration, and you build it in an hour of normal coding: accept when the gray text matches your intent, reject without guilt when it doesn't. Treat every acceptance as a micro code review.

## Inline edit: Cmd+K

Select code, press **Cmd+K** (Ctrl+K), describe the change in plain language — "convert to async/await," "add input validation with helpful errors" — and review the proposed diff before accepting. It's scoped, visible, and reversible, which makes it the ideal training ground for the review habits that matter much more in Step 2.

Do this now: take any project, spend 30 minutes coding normally with Tab on, then make five Cmd+K edits of increasing ambition. Note where the model shines and where it invents things — that map of strengths and failure modes is the real deliverable.

---

## Check yourself

You're done when your VS Code setup works inside Cursor, you've accepted and rejected enough Tab suggestions to feel the calibration, and every Cmd+K diff you accepted was one you actually read.
