## About
Vercel is the fastest way to put a frontend project on the internet. Connect a GitHub repository, and every push deploys automatically — with HTTPS, a global CDN, and a shareable URL, all configured for you. It's the platform behind Next.js, and the default choice for shipping React apps, portfolios, and AI app frontends.

This guide gets you from "it runs on localhost" to "here's my link" — and then teaches you the judgment part: what Vercel's free tier actually covers, where the costs hide, and when a different platform is the right call.

## Why Vercel specifically?

For a student shipping a frontend, the pitch is simple: it's the lowest-friction path from repo to URL, and the URL is what goes on your resume. Recruiters click links; they don't clone repos. Vercel also happens to be what a large share of industry frontend teams deploy on, so the workflow you learn — git push, preview deploy, review, promote — is the workflow you'll see at work.

The tradeoff: Vercel is frontend-and-serverless shaped. Long-running backends, websockets, and databases live better elsewhere (that's what our [Render guide](/aideveloperguide/render) is for). Knowing which shape your project is — that's the skill.
